How it works

From one click to a Saturday brief

You save things whenever you find them. Nothing happens to them right away. When your issue is due, one scheduled run reads the whole pile, groups what belongs together, and writes your five takeaways and a two-host podcast. Here is each stage and what it runs on.

The pipeline, in five stages

  1. 01

    Capture

    You click save in the Chrome extension, or paste a link or text on the site.

  2. →
  3. 02

    Store

    The app API saves the item and its raw text: the article body, video captions or what you pasted.

  4. →
  5. 03

    Wait

    Saves collect in Triggerware tables, one set per brain. Nothing is processed one by one.

  6. →
  7. 04

    Digest

    A scheduled Triggerware trigger fires. An agent on Claude groups the saves and writes the issue and a two-host script. Text-to-speech renders the two voices.

  8. →
  9. 05

    Deliver

    The issue goes out by email and in the app, following each member's own settings.

Built on

Digo app-starter

github.com/digohq/app-starter

The app around the brain. It gives us the Next.js web app, the NestJS and Prisma API, sign-in by email code or Google, workspaces with Owner, Admin and Member roles, and email plus in-app notifications. That left our time for the part that is new: saving and the digest.

Triggerware SDKs

github.com/triggerware/tw2-sdks

The pipeline. Saves land in Triggerware tables, and a scheduled trigger fires at each brain's delivery time. That run hands the pile to the agent, which writes the issue and the podcast script. No item is processed on its own.

Privacy

  • Saves happen only when you click. Nothing is captured automatically.
  • The extension never reads pages you do not save.
  • Workspaces are private to their members.
